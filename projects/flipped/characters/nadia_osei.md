# Character: NADIA OSEI

**Role:** Protagonist
**Age:** 34
**Appearance:** Sharp, economical — someone who moves through the world taking up exactly the space she intends to and no more. She dresses practically, not plainly. Her stillness reads to some people as coldness. It is actually attention.

---

## VOICE

Nadia speaks in complete sentences and waits for you to finish yours. She asks clarifying questions rather than interpretive ones — she wants data, not impressions. In emotional situations she becomes quieter, not louder. This is sometimes mistaken for detachment. It is how she processes.

**Sample line:**
*"I'm not saying you're lying. I'm saying there's a gap between what you're describing and what I'm observing, and I'd like to understand it."*

---

## WANT vs. NEED

- **Want:** To expose McGee — to make what he is doing visible, legible, and stoppable before Carol or someone else like her doesn't survive.
- **Need:** To stop using her brother's death as a debt she owes the world. She cannot save Marcus. She can only decide what she does next.

---

## FLAW

Nadia trusts her analysis over her instincts, which means she sometimes waits for certainty when action was what was needed. She was doing this when Marcus died — gathering information, watching for patterns, not wanting to overreact. She has never said this out loud to anyone.

---

## BACKSTORY

Nadia completed a master's degree in behavioral psychology before pivoting to data analytics — she found the clinical world "too comfortable with ambiguity." She and her brother Marcus were close in the specific way of two people who grew up as each other's primary emotional support. When he died, she didn't fall apart. She built a spreadsheet of everything she remembered him saying in the last year. Looking for the pattern she missed.

She found a name in his journal: a therapist in McGee's extended referral network.

She enrolled in McGee's practice telling herself it was research. She is not fully honest with herself about how much of it is grief.

---

## RELATIONSHIPS

- **Marcus (deceased):** The engine under everything. She is not grieving in sessions with McGee — she is investigating. But the grief is real, and McGee finds it immediately.
- **Carol:** The patient whose deterioration galvanizes Nadia from analyst to actor. Carol is what Nadia could become if she let the process continue. Carol is also, in some way, a version of Marcus — someone she might still be in time to reach.
- **McGee:** The most complicated relationship in the film. Nadia understands him better than anyone has. This means she also, on some level, can see what he needed to become this. She does not forgive it. But she sees it.

---

## ARC

Nadia begins the film as an observer with a hypothesis. She ends it as someone who chose to act — imperfectly, without a guaranteed outcome, without waiting until she was certain. That is her change. She does something before she knows if it will work.

---

## CONTRADICTIONS

- She is in genuine pain and refuses to let McGee help her with it — even though, tactically, letting him in would give her better intelligence.
- She is more comfortable dismantling McGee's system than sitting with the question of why she is the one who needed to do it.
- She does not consider herself brave. She is doing this because the alternative is living with having seen it and done nothing. She calls that calculation, not courage.
