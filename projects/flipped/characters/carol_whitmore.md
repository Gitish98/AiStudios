# Character: CAROL WHITMORE

**Role:** Supporting — the cost of inaction made human
**Age:** 47
**Appearance:** The kind of person who was probably warm and easy-laughing once. Still tries to be. The effort is visible now.

---

## VOICE

Carol speaks with the particular cadence of someone repeating language they've been given — McGee's phrases have migrated into her daily speech. She says things like *"I'm still in the process"* and *"the resistance is information"* the way a person recites a prayer: not thinking about the words, only the feeling of saying them.

**Sample line:**
*"I know it's hard to understand if you're not as far along as I am. Brian says that's normal. The people closest to us are often the most threatened by our growth."*

---

## FUNCTION IN THE STORY

Carol is not a victim in a simple sense — she is a devotee, and her devotion is genuine. She loves what McGee's framework has given her: a language for her pain, a community, a sense of forward motion. That none of it is real does not make her experience of it less real.

She is the answer to the question: *what does McGee's method look like at full completion?*

She is also the reason Nadia cannot stay in analyst mode. Watching Carol use the language of healing to describe symptoms of collapse is the moment the film stops being intellectual and becomes personal.

---

## BACKSTORY

Carol came to McGee three years ago after a divorce. She was functional, if unhappy. She is now fully embedded in his ecosystem — intensive sessions twice a week, volunteer work for his foundation, a small group of friends who are all McGee patients. Her adult daughter has stopped calling.

---

## ARC

Carol's arc is largely offscreen — we see her in two scenes, and the distance between them is the arc. In the first she is troubled but present. By the time of her hospitalization she has been moved entirely inside the framework.

Whether she recovers after the film ends is deliberately unresolved.
