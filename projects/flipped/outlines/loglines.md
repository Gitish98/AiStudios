# Flipped — Logline Variations

---

**VARIATION 1 — The Victim's Perspective (Recommended)**
When a grieving mother enrolls in Dr. Brian McGee's celebrated recovery program, she slowly realizes his methods aren't healing her — they're systematically dismantling her — and that millions of Americans are paying him to be broken.

*Angle: Ground-level. Personal stakes. The audience discovers the fraud as she does.*

---

**VARIATION 2 — The Reckoning**
America's most trusted therapist has built a $200-million empire teaching people how to heal — but one patient, a former cognitive scientist, recognizes his techniques as a precise system of psychological warfare, and decides the only way to stop him is to use his methods against him.

*Angle: Puts the power shift front and center. Sets up the "flip" explicitly.*

---

**VARIATION 3 — The Monster in Plain Sight**
Dr. Brian McGee has 40 million podcast listeners, three bestselling books, and a waiting list of desperate patients — and every single one of them is being quietly engineered toward their darkest moment, because a destabilized patient is a loyal one.

*Angle: Opens from McGee's side. More unsettling — positions him as the POV character.*

---

**VARIATION 4 — The Short Film Logline (Tight / Punchy)**
A celebrated therapist's star patient finally understands his method: he doesn't heal people. He owns them.

*Angle: Minimal. Lets the premise breathe. Best for pitch documents and festival submissions.*

---

**SELECTED LOGLINE FOR PROJECT:**
America's most trusted therapist has built an empire on healing — but his methods are a precise system of psychological warfare, and the one patient sharp enough to see through it is also the most dangerous person he's ever put in a chair.
