# Flipped — Beat Sheet (Save the Cat)
**Genre:** Psychological Thriller (Short Film — ~22 pages)
**Logline:** America's most trusted therapist has built an empire on healing — but his methods are a precise system of psychological warfare, and the one patient sharp enough to see through it is also the most dangerous person he's ever put in a chair.

---

## THE 15 BEATS

---

### 1. OPENING IMAGE (p. 1)
A sea of faces — tears, relief, nodding heads — watching Dr. Brian McGee command a stage. He is warm, certain, magnetic. A standing ovation. The camera holds on one face in the crowd that isn't clapping: **NADIA OSEI**, 34, still, watching him like she's reading a document rather than witnessing a performance.

*Establishes: the world McGee has built, and the one person in it who sees it differently.*

---

### 2. THEME STATED (p. 2)
McGee, backstage after the seminar, to his assistant: *"People don't want to be fixed. They want to feel like someone finally understands how broken they are."*

He means it as a compliment to his work. It is also his entire method, spoken aloud without shame.

*The theme: the most dangerous manipulation is the kind that feels like love.*

---

### 3. SET-UP (pp. 2–5)
We meet Nadia six weeks earlier, arriving at McGee's private practice for her first session. She lost her younger brother to suicide eight months ago. She's been referred by three different people — McGee's reach is that wide. His office is designed to feel like safety: warm light, no sharp edges, a soft hum of ambient sound.

McGee runs his initial intake. He is exceptional at this — reading her, reflecting her pain back to her with surgical precision. Nadia is smart and guarded, but grief has left a gap in the armor.

We also establish the world around McGee: his podcast, his book *You Were Always Whole*, his inner circle of "graduate patients" who now work as his unpaid evangelists.

---

### 4. CATALYST (p. 6)
During their third session, Nadia — who holds a master's degree in behavioral psychology, a detail she hasn't shared with McGee — recognizes a specific technique he uses: **strategic negative reframing**. He's not helping her process her brother's death. He's subtly attributing blame to her. Incrementally. Session by session.

She goes home and pulls her old textbooks.

---

### 5. DEBATE (pp. 7–8)
Nadia debates whether she's seeing what she thinks she's seeing. Maybe she's projecting. Maybe grief is distorting her read. She attends a support group for his patients — all of them devoted, all of them worse than they were a year ago, none of them able to see it.

One woman, **CAROL**, mentions she's been having thoughts she's never had before. She calls them "gifts from the process." Nadia recognizes them as ideation.

The debate ends. This is real.

---

### 6. BREAK INTO TWO (p. 9)
Nadia makes a decision: she won't quit. She won't report him — she has no hard evidence, and McGee has lawyers, loyalists, and a public persona worth hundreds of millions of dollars. Instead, she goes back into the chair.

But this time she takes notes.

---

### 7. B STORY (pp. 9–11)
The B story lives inside McGee himself. Brief scenes — almost clinical — reveal his private self: the practiced laugh he runs through before sessions, the spreadsheet where he tracks patient "dependency metrics," a phone call with a pharmaceutical partner. He is not a monster who doesn't know what he's doing. He knows exactly what he's doing.

One scene: he watches a video of himself on stage and genuinely admires it. He believes he is providing something real. That is what makes him dangerous.

---

### 8. FUN AND GAMES (pp. 11–15)
The promise of the premise fully delivered: Nadia plays the patient while dissecting the therapist.

A series of sessions, each more revealing than the last:
- She begins mirroring his techniques back at him — feeding him the emotional responses he's engineered to need
- She discovers his intake forms contain questions designed to identify the most isolated, most suggestible patients — they get escalated to "intensive care" (more sessions, higher fees, deeper dependency)
- She finds Carol has been moved to intensive care

The audience sees both levels simultaneously: what McGee thinks is happening, and what is actually happening. It is almost darkly comic — except Carol is real, and getting worse.

---

### 9. MIDPOINT (p. 15)
False victory: Nadia has enough to confront him. She does — obliquely, in session, describing "a hypothetical patient" whose therapist she suspects of misconduct. She watches McGee's face.

He doesn't flinch. He smiles, and turns the hypothetical back on her. *"What does it mean to you that you're protecting this imaginary patient instead of yourself?"*

He knows. He's known for a while. And he isn't afraid of her.

The stakes double: this isn't just about evidence. He will preemptively destroy her credibility if she moves against him. He has the playbook for that too.

---

### 10. BAD GUYS CLOSE IN (pp. 16–17)
McGee activates his network. A smear begins quietly — former patients (loyal ones, broken ones) start posting about a "disturbed woman" in his practice spreading paranoid accusations. Nadia's employer gets an anonymous letter questioning her mental stability. Her intake forms, reframed, paint her as a delusional, grief-consumed woman with a vendetta against the man trying to help her.

He is turning his methods on her at scale.

---

### 11. ALL IS LOST (p. 18)
Carol attempts suicide. She survives — but barely. When Nadia tries to reach her, Carol's family tells her to stay away. McGee has already been there, already positioned himself as the one trying to save Carol from "outside interference."

Nadia sits in her car outside Carol's hospital and understands the full picture: she has no evidence that would survive a legal challenge, no allies who haven't already been turned, and a man with infinite resources who is now actively hunting her credibility.

She has one session left before he will almost certainly terminate her as a patient and complete the smear.

---

### 12. DARK NIGHT OF THE SOUL (p. 18–19)
Nadia almost walks away. She pulls up her brother's last text messages. Reads them. She has wondered for eight months whether she missed the signs. She now understands: he was seeing a therapist in McGee's extended network.

This was never just about her.

---

### 13. BREAK INTO THREE (p. 19)
She walks into the final session with one thing McGee doesn't know she has: a recording of Carol — made weeks earlier, with consent — describing the specific techniques, the specific language, the specific sequence of sessions. Not a legal case. Something more immediate.

She's going to make him say it out loud.

---

### 14. FINALE (pp. 20–22)
The final session. Two people who both know what is happening, neither acknowledging it.

Nadia walks McGee — through careful, precise, mirrored use of his own techniques — into articulating his philosophy without the euphemisms. She reflects his language back to him, escalates his certainty, feeds his need to be understood by someone truly intelligent.

And he gives it to her. Not a confession — something more damning. A thesis. *"Stability is a lie people tell themselves. My patients are more alive in their instability than they ever were before."*

She stops the recording on her phone. Sets it on the table between them.

McGee looks at it. Looks at her. The warmth drains from his face — and for the first time, we see what lives underneath it.

She stands, picks up the phone, and walks out.

*What she does with the recording is not shown. That's not the story.*

---

### 15. FINAL IMAGE (p. 22)
McGee sits alone in his office. Same warm light, same ambient hum. He straightens his jacket. Picks up his phone. Dials.

*"Clear my afternoon,"* he says. *"I need to make some calls."*

He is already building the next move. He will not be stopped by this. The machine is too large.

But the camera stays on his face — and for just a moment, before the mask returns, we see it: not fear. Something smaller. The faint, unfamiliar outline of being known.

*FADE TO BLACK.*

**FLIPPED**

## STRUCTURAL NOTES

- **Runtime target:** 20–25 minutes
- **Locations:** McGee's office (primary), seminar hall (opening), Nadia's apartment, hospital exterior
- **Core tension engine:** Two intelligent people in the same room, each running a strategy the other can partially see
- **Tonal reference:** *The Conversation* (Coppola) meets *Nightcrawler* — clinical dread, not jump scares
- **What makes McGee the most dangerous person in the room:** He is not a villain who hates his victims. He genuinely believes he is providing something valuable. His self-deception is total and reinforced by success. There is no appeal to conscience because his conscience is clean.
